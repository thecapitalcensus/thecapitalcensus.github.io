Verification Ledger

Every test we ran, every proposition we adopted, and every governance failure we caught — including results unfavourable to our own hypotheses, and errors made by our own referees.

We publish this because the ledger is the work. This project offers measurement and disclosure, not performance. Roles below are abbreviated as supervisor (referee), implementer (execution), and principal (final authority); how the work is actually carried out, including the use of AI tools, is set out in Methodology §7.

Part 2 — Adopted propositions

  1. The investable GMP has no performance advantage over 60/40. Fixed weights fall short; the variant specifications are indistinguishable. Eighteen years, 219 months, all regimes: zero evidence of superiority.
  2. Currency exposure on the bond side is a variable to be chosen deliberately. In the high-inflation, strong-dollar regime it was worth +0.215%/month (p = 0.0075), cumulatively +1.75% in 2021–22 — then the series' only statistically robust finding. Superseded in scale by B6, which did not confirm it; retained for the record.
  3. The account of gold as an inflation shield cannot be confirmed in any then-feasible form. It depends on the one-off revaluation as gold left its official price in 1971–74. Prohibited in all public claims.
  4. Preservation of real purchasing power is not guaranteed even by global diversification. Real maximum drawdown in 1968–82 exceeded 30%. The honest ceiling is an uncertain but positive-expected-value store of value, as against the certain erosion of zero-yield deposits.
  5. There is no unique “correct” set of GMP weights. External estimates put equity anywhere from 36% to 50%; our own v0 and v1 missed the same primary data by ±12pt in opposite directions. Convergence is a function of filter calibration, not an approach to truth.
  6. The IG-credit offset between our estimate and index-eligible estimates has been stable at +5.8 to +6.4pt for three years. A consistent fixed difference reflecting what is measured — investor holdings versus index membership. Explainability of divergence, not its smallness, is the quality criterion.
  7. The frozen methodology did not break on two unseen years. Two hold-outs passed, direction tests 6/6 — but these are six correlated binary outcomes, and no statistical power is claimed.
  8. Fifty rulebook-compliant years change nothing: no performance advantage over 60/40 (B6). At equal risk the two are indistinguishable (+0.04pt/yr, p = 0.935). The GMP is not an allocation that loses; it is an allocation that takes less risk.
  9. The Sharpe advantage seen in early versions was the contribution of contrarian rebalancing toward fixed weights, not of market-portfolio composition (B6). The two must not be conflated when explaining diversification.
  10. Following market capitalisation structurally includes buying high (B6). In January 1980 the rules held 12.9% gold; in January 1990, 35.6% equities. Not a defect but a definition — and one that must be disclosed.
  11. “Neither wins nor loses” can only be asserted as a bound, never as an identity (B6, updated by B6.1). What the data support is: at 90% confidence the annualized equal-risk difference versus 60/40 lies within ±0.88pt/yr — the least favourable of the three benchmark definitions examined (base definition ±0.81pt; US Treasuries only ±0.83pt; local-currency bonds ±0.88pt). We no longer report p-values against an equivalence margin of our own choosing (amendment 5): an interval estimate is dual to the family of TOST outcomes at every margin at once, so reporting the interval removes the freedom to select one. “The difference is zero” can never be claimed from any sample. Note the asymmetry: the central-bank correction improved returns, which moved the point estimate away from zero and worsened the bound from ±0.75 to ±0.81pt. Corrections do not necessarily strengthen one's claims.
  12. Regime neutrality distinguishes the GMP from single-asset concentration, but not from 60/40 or equal weights (B6). The differentiator is provenance — the aggregate holdings of all investors — not outcomes. Its high-inflation risk-adjusted rank was fifth of six; inflation resilience must never be a selling point.
  13. Market-cap gold holdings capture almost none of gold's upside (B7). Even in a decade when gold rose 335%, an average 3.5% weight contributed +0.56pt/yr against the equity underweight's −2.52pt. The GMP must not be suggested as a way to express conviction in gold.
  14. “No negative real ten-year window in fifty years (worst +0.08%)” is true — but not unique to the GMP (60/40: also 100%), barely positive, and USD-denominated against U.S. CPI (B7).
  15. Choosing among diversified portfolios is choosing how much risk to take, not which is better (B7). All four lie nearly on one line in risk–return space (GMP–60/40 correlation 0.944); differentiation by outcome quality is impossible, and rank stability favours the one-third allocation. What remains is provenance — weights chosen by no one.

Part 3 — Governance events

A model-risk case: the annual approximation reversed by monthly data

B1 (annual granularity) and B2 (monthly, actual instruments) reached opposite conclusions. Lesson adopted: coarse-grained tests must not drive decisions.

A confounded proxy: the shortfall was currency, not allocation

B2's shortfall was driven primarily by unhedged currency exposure, not by the asset mix. Lesson: confirm that what is being tested matches what is being specified — before testing.

Pre-registration with teeth

v2 passed its gates 3/3 and was still not adopted, because a registered rule said an uncalibrated parameter blocks adoption. v3 was likewise not adopted over a parameter whose influence is only ±0.6pt. Rules earn their keep precisely when they hurt.

Gate revision after seeing results: legitimate and contaminated at once

After viewing v1's results, the gates were revised to like-for-like universes and matched dates. The supervisor recorded, in its own words, that the revision favoured our engine — and published the old gates alongside the new. A revision can be methodologically correct and still void the protections of pre-registration. Both facts are on the record.

A concept change after results

The redefinition A9 → A9′ was decided after v2's results were seen. It was justified on theory alone — consistency with a prior design decision — and never on outcomes. The prohibition on outcome-based justification stands.

Referee contamination: how we caught and contained it

Through a procedure-design error, the supervisor received the 2025 reference values before scoring. The principal caught it. Containment: recusal, a mechanical rule (widen the range rather than pick a point), and scoring restricted to machine comparison. The 2025 hold-out was downgraded to quasi-clean. Lesson: the referee is also subject to audit.

Establishing implementer blinding

An instruction to pass reference values to the implementer was withdrawn — flagged by the principal — and the process was redesigned around freezing, hashing, and blind execution. Executor and referee were separated from that point on. One related fact is recorded plainly: a hash pre-commitment was proposed and, by the principal's decision, omitted; the hold-out ratings state “no pre-commitment” accordingly, and accurate labelling substitutes for the missing mechanism.

A defect in the freeze

The frozen vintage code turned out to embed a v2 build: the frozen artefact was not the whole execution path. It was resolved with a disclosed out-of-freeze connection module, and the 2024 rating carries the exception note. Lesson: verify the freeze across the entire execution path, not the file list.

The referee's wrong assumption — and why the error ran against us

The supervisor assumed a first-quarter as-of date for the 2024 external reference; the true date was end-June, and the principal's information was correct. The three-month misalignment was quantified and noted as biasing against the engine. Handled within the registered protocol.

When the implementer was right to disobey

An instruction to deduct recycled gold was not executed. The implementer documented that it conflicted with the inventory definition in use, and the deviation was approved as a correction of a flawed instruction. Instructions are also subject to audit.

An amendment that failed on its own terms

Amendment 1 sought to calibrate the high-yield share using a manually obtained industry workbook, after automated retrieval had been blocked. The workbook turned out to report issuance — a flow — where the parameter needs amounts outstanding, a stock. The gates passed 3/3 and the machine judgment nonetheless returned adopt = false, because the parameter remained uncalibrated. That is the second occasion on which a registered rule cost us something. The episode also exposed a structural fact: the three top-level gate buckets are invariant, by construction, to any parameter that only moves allocation within a bucket. Whether a proposed parameter crosses a bucket boundary must now be classified in advance.

A proposal we could not claim was disinterested

Amendment 3 moved official publication to the granularity at which the underlying statistics actually measure — seven sleeves, with the investment-grade and high-yield split demoted to an auxiliary, uncalibrated estimate. We record without qualification that the proposal was made after the calibration route was known to be closed, and is therefore inseparable from a motive to avoid an inconvenient obligation. It was adopted on primary evidence instead: the international debt statistics carry no rating dimension, and the industry body does not publish the split — both confirmed independently, after the proposal was made.

Two copies of the truth

A discrepancy over whether the index has seven or eight sleeves was reported by the principal. The cause was not a typo. Amendments processed on the drafting side had never been transferred to the code repository, and the two sides had been diverging document by document. A separate defect surfaced in the same investigation: an unlabelled artefact file from the ten-sleeve v0 era was still in circulation. Resolution: a single canonical repository, an auto-generated manifest that detects documents it has not received, a sleeve history generated from the actual files, and the canonical phrasing “eight computed, seven published”. Lesson: a document set without a defined original will diverge, and no one will be able to say which copy is wrong.

Choosing the margin after seeing the answer

Our equivalence claim was originally stated against a fixed margin of ±1.0pt per year. The script that produced it did not hold a single margin: it looped over a grid of 0.5, 1.0, 1.5 and 2.0, and the value carried into this ledger was the smallest grid point that passed. The next smaller point failed. No margin had been registered before the test, and no economic rationale for 1.0pt was recorded anywhere in our documents or code. The second run inherited the margin unchanged, so the selection happened once and was not repeated — which limits the damage without excusing it. We withdrew the margin-dependent p-values and replaced them with the interval, which does not depend on a margin at all (amendment 5). Lesson adopted: a test threshold is a parameter, and pre-registration must cover it. Reported by the principal.

Generalising a result from a window it was not measured on

A draft of our paper stated, as a general fact, that the sign of the difference reverses when the benchmark's bond leg is restricted to US Treasuries. That reversal had been observed on B2 — a shorter window with fixed weights and a different design — and had never been tested on the fifty-year window. We attributed it correctly, pre-registered a sensitivity run with a hash committed before execution, and carried it out (amendment 6). The reversal was confirmed on the full window; none of the three variants produces a difference distinguishable from zero, each point estimate being under a quarter of its own standard error. Lesson: a result belongs to the window it was measured on. What can be said is that the sign carries no information, not that either sign is right. Reported by the principal.

A bound we rounded in our own favour

The sensitivity run turned up something we had not been looking for. The bound we had been publishing as “within ±0.80pt” has an exact value of 0.8041; the stated figure was rounded down, and downward is the direction that flatters us. The same phrasing also quoted the most favourable of the three benchmark definitions without saying so. Both were corrected (amendment 7): the public figure is now ±0.88pt, the least favourable of the three, and a rounding rule was added — every bound in public material is rounded up, never down. Found by the implementer's own check.

A rule that was recorded but not binding

After the margin episode we wrote the lesson into this ledger and considered the matter closed. It was not: the rulebook's pre-registration clause still said only that criteria must be fixed before execution, without saying which criteria. The gap that produced the original failure was still open in the document that actually binds us. The clause now enumerates four mandatory items — significance level, equivalence margin, window length, and lag-selection rule — and prohibits promoting a grid-searched value to an adopted one (amendment 8). Lesson: a lesson belongs in the rulebook, not in a footnote; footnotes do not bind. Reported by the principal, as a requirement of the paper.

The published page and the original disagreed

A decision taken on 28 August — that JPY-denominated re-runs and one further comparison were out of scope — had been applied to this page but never written back into the internal ledger, which still carried both as open items. The public copy was ahead of the original. We resolved it in favour of this page and corrected the original (amendment 9), and added a stated criterion for what belongs in the public list, so that an omission can be told apart from an oversight. Lesson: a decision goes into the original first. If the published copy leads, no one can tell whether the original is stale or the publication is wrong.

A retired scope that kept reappearing — and the check we should have had

Four days after JPY denomination was placed out of scope, three statements assuming it were still alive in the internal ledger: a JPY figure in the B6 row, an open registration for a JPY run, and a proposition conditioned on a JPY verification that will now never happen. All three were removed (amendment 10). The pattern across the six events above is a single one. Our numbers are protected by hashes, manifests, frozen files and confirmed-value files; our prose was protected by memory alone, and the same fact was copied by hand into six documents. We now run a document linter that detects retired terminology, superseded figures and withdrawn concepts across every current document, and a document is not current until it passes. Lesson: if a decision is enforced by remembering it, it will survive in the places no one thought to look.

Part 5 — Claims we prohibit ourselves from making

The following are prohibited in every public material we produce. They are listed here as prohibitions, not as claims.

  1. Performance superiority over 60/40 or any benchmark.
  2. Categorical statements that the index never trails the market average.
  3. Inflation resilience argued from the 1970s.
  4. Guarantee-like claims about preserving real purchasing power.
  5. Deposit- or savings-like connotations.
  6. Gold as a permanent portfolio stabiliser.
  7. “The longer you hold, the better” — the twenty-year-window advantage is 0.8%.
  8. Explanations that conflate rebalancing effects with market-portfolio theory.
  9. Suggesting the GMP as a vehicle for conviction in gold; a 3.5% market-cap weight delivers none of it (B7).

Four statements are permitted verbatim, and only verbatim:

“The index takes no discretionary bets. Its weights are the aggregate holdings of all investors, chosen by no one.”

“All rules, calibrations, and known limitations are disclosed in full, including results unfavorable to our own hypotheses.”

“Cost minimization is the only improvement that works with certainty.”

“Over our 50-year verification (1976–2026, 606 months, USD, gross of all costs), the annualized return difference versus a 60/40 portfolio was within ±0.88 percentage points at equal risk (90% confidence interval). This is the least favourable of the three 60/40 bond-leg definitions we examined.”

Rounding rule. Every bound used in public material is rounded up, never down. Rounding down is the direction that favours us and is prohibited.

The full standards, including phrase-level examples of what each prohibition rules out, are maintained as an internal document and applied to every page before publication.
