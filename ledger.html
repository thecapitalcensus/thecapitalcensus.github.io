Part 2 — Adopted propositions

  1. The investable GMP has no performance advantage over 60/40. Fixed weights fall short; the variant specifications are equivalent. Eighteen years, 219 months, all regimes: zero evidence of superiority.
  2. Currency exposure on the bond side is a variable to be chosen deliberately. In the high-inflation, strong-dollar regime it was worth +0.215%/month (p = 0.0075), cumulatively +1.75% in 2021–22 — then the series' only statistically robust finding. Superseded in scale by B6, which did not confirm it; retained for the record.
  3. The account of gold as an inflation shield cannot be confirmed in any then-feasible form. It depends on the one-off revaluation as gold left its official price in 1971–74. Prohibited in all public claims.
  4. Preservation of real purchasing power is not guaranteed even by global diversification. Real maximum drawdown in 1968–82 exceeded 30%. The honest ceiling is an uncertain but positive-expected-value store of value, as against the certain erosion of zero-yield deposits.
  5. There is no unique “correct” set of GMP weights. External estimates put equity anywhere from 36% to 50%; our own v0 and v1 missed the same primary data by ±12pt in opposite directions. Convergence is a function of filter calibration, not an approach to truth.
  6. The IG-credit offset between our estimate and index-eligible estimates has been stable at +5.8 to +6.4pt for three years. A consistent fixed difference reflecting what is measured — investor holdings versus index membership. Explainability of divergence, not its smallness, is the quality criterion.
  7. The frozen methodology did not break on two unseen years. Two hold-outs passed, direction tests 6/6 — but these are six correlated binary outcomes, and no statistical power is claimed.
  8. Fifty rulebook-compliant years change nothing: no performance advantage over 60/40 (B6). At equal risk the two are indistinguishable (+0.04pt/yr, p = 0.935). The GMP is not an allocation that loses; it is an allocation that takes less risk.
  9. The Sharpe advantage seen in early versions was the contribution of contrarian rebalancing toward fixed weights, not of market-portfolio composition (B6). The two must not be conflated when explaining diversification.
  10. Following market capitalisation structurally includes buying high (B6). In January 1980 the rules held 12.9% gold; in January 1990, 35.6% equities. Not a defect but a definition — and one that must be disclosed.
  11. “Neither wins nor loses” can only be asserted through an equivalence test (B6, updated by B6.1). 90% confidence interval ±0.80pt/yr; TOST p = 0.0177 at ±1.0pt bounds. “The difference is zero” can never be claimed from any sample. Note the asymmetry: the central-bank correction improved returns, which moved the point estimate away from zero and worsened the bound from ±0.74 to ±0.80pt. Corrections do not necessarily strengthen one's claims.
  12. Regime neutrality distinguishes the GMP from single-asset concentration, but not from 60/40 or equal weights (B6). The differentiator is provenance — the aggregate holdings of all investors — not outcomes. Its high-inflation risk-adjusted rank was fifth of six; inflation resilience must never be a selling point.
  13. Market-cap gold holdings capture almost none of gold's upside (B7). Even in a decade when gold rose 335%, an average 3.5% weight contributed +0.56pt/yr against the equity underweight's −2.52pt. The GMP must not be suggested as a way to express conviction in gold.
  14. “No negative real ten-year window in fifty years (worst +0.08%)” is true — but not unique to the GMP (60/40: also 100%), barely positive, and USD-denominated against U.S. CPI (B7).
  15. Choosing among diversified portfolios is choosing how much risk to take, not which is better (B7). All four lie nearly on one line in risk–return space (GMP–60/40 correlation 0.944); differentiation by outcome quality is impossible, and rank stability favours the one-third allocation. What remains is provenance — weights chosen by no one.

Part 3 — Governance events

A model-risk case: the annual approximation reversed by monthly data

B1 (annual granularity) and B2 (monthly, actual instruments) reached opposite conclusions. Lesson adopted: coarse-grained tests must not drive decisions.

A confounded proxy: the shortfall was currency, not allocation

B2's shortfall was driven primarily by unhedged currency exposure, not by the asset mix. Lesson: confirm that what is being tested matches what is being specified — before testing.

Pre-registration with teeth

v2 passed its gates 3/3 and was still not adopted, because a registered rule said an uncalibrated parameter blocks adoption. v3 was likewise not adopted over a parameter whose influence is only ±0.6pt. Rules earn their keep precisely when they hurt.

Gate revision after seeing results: legitimate and contaminated at once

After viewing v1's results, the gates were revised to like-for-like universes and matched dates. The supervisor recorded, in its own words, that the revision favoured our engine — and published the old gates alongside the new. A revision can be methodologically correct and still void the protections of pre-registration. Both facts are on the record.

A concept change after results

The redefinition A9 → A9′ was decided after v2's results were seen. It was justified on theory alone — consistency with a prior design decision — and never on outcomes. The prohibition on outcome-based justification stands.

Referee contamination: how we caught and contained it

Through a procedure-design error, the supervisor received the 2025 reference values before scoring. The principal caught it. Containment: recusal, a mechanical rule (widen the range rather than pick a point), and scoring restricted to machine comparison. The 2025 hold-out was downgraded to quasi-clean. Lesson: the referee is also subject to audit.

Establishing implementer blinding

An instruction to pass reference values to the implementer was withdrawn — flagged by the principal — and the process was redesigned around freezing, hashing, and blind execution. Executor and referee were separated from that point on. One related fact is recorded plainly: a hash pre-commitment was proposed and, by the principal's decision, omitted; the hold-out ratings state “no pre-commitment” accordingly, and accurate labelling substitutes for the missing mechanism.

A defect in the freeze

The frozen vintage code turned out to embed a v2 build: the frozen artefact was not the whole execution path. It was resolved with a disclosed out-of-freeze connection module, and the 2024 rating carries the exception note. Lesson: verify the freeze across the entire execution path, not the file list.

The referee's wrong assumption — and why the error ran against us

The supervisor assumed a first-quarter as-of date for the 2024 external reference; the true date was end-June, and the principal's information was correct. The three-month misalignment was quantified and noted as biasing against the engine. Handled within the registered protocol.

When the implementer was right to disobey

An instruction to deduct recycled gold was not executed. The implementer documented that it conflicted with the inventory definition in use, and the deviation was approved as a correction of a flawed instruction. Instructions are also subject to audit.

Part 5 — Claims we prohibit ourselves from making

The following are prohibited in every public material we produce. They are listed here as prohibitions, not as claims.

  1. Performance superiority over 60/40 or any benchmark.
  2. Categorical statements that the index never trails the market average.
  3. Inflation resilience argued from the 1970s.
  4. Guarantee-like claims about preserving real purchasing power.
  5. Deposit- or savings-like connotations.
  6. Gold as a permanent portfolio stabiliser.
  7. “The longer you hold, the better” — the twenty-year-window advantage is 0.8%.
  8. Explanations that conflate rebalancing effects with market-portfolio theory.
  9. Suggesting the GMP as a vehicle for conviction in gold; a 3.5% market-cap weight delivers none of it (B7).

Four statements are permitted verbatim, and only verbatim:

“The index takes no discretionary bets. Its weights are the aggregate holdings of all investors, chosen by no one.”

“All rules, calibrations, and known limitations are disclosed in full, including results unfavorable to our own hypotheses.”

“Cost minimization is the only improvement that works with certainty.”

“Over our 50-year verification (1976–2026), the annualized return difference versus a 60/40 portfolio was within ±0.80 percentage points at equal risk (90% confidence interval; equivalence not superiority — TOST p = 0.0177 at ±1.0pt bounds).”

The full standards, including phrase-level examples of what each prohibition rules out, are maintained as an internal document and applied to every page before publication.
